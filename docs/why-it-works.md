# Why it works

<p class="answer" markdown>**Why does Supportive Stranger work the way it does?** It is built on one premise: people process difficult emotions better when they can **put them into words, see them from the outside, and then let them go**. The app combines three established practices – expressive writing, externalisation (from art therapy) and a deliberate closing ritual – into one short interaction, with an anonymous, non-judging stranger in the middle.</p>

The emotional arc of every reflection:

1. **Naming** – you put a vague, pressing feeling into words.
2. **Being witnessed** – an anonymous stranger responds with warmth, without judgment or advice.
3. **Seeing it from the outside** – the feeling becomes a scene you look at instead of live in.
4. **Releasing** – you deliberately destroy the scene, closing the loop.

## 1. Expressive writing

Writing about an emotional experience – even briefly, even without sharing it – has measurable benefits. James Pennebaker's research on expressive writing showed that putting distress into language reduces its intensity. Part of the mechanism is **labelling**: naming an emotion engages the reflective parts of the mind and quiets the reactive ones.

How the app applies it:

- **Entries are short (100 words maximum).** The goal is the first honest sentence, not a full journal page. Brevity lowers the barrier and keeps you in the feeling instead of narrating around it.
- **Prompts never lead.** A leading prompt pulls you toward a socially acceptable answer. The [sort-it-out prompts](features/sort-it-out.md) only help you locate what is already there: they categorise, they don't suggest content.
- **Labelling is built in.** Choosing *when*, *what about* and *how it feels* is itself a way of naming.

## 2. Externalisation (art therapy)

Turning an inner state into an outside object is at the heart of art therapy. It creates distance: a feeling you are *inside* is overwhelming; a feeling you can *look at* is manageable.

How the app applies it:

- **A scene, not an abstraction.** The [illustration](features/the-illustration.md) shows a figure – you – in a setting, seen from a small distance.
- **Made from your own words**, so it feels like yours, not generic. Recognising it – *"yes, that's how it feels"* – is the moment that matters.
- **Universal, not stereotyped.** The scene carries the emotional atmosphere, not a literal picture of your circumstances. That's why the categories you tap are never used for the image.

## 3. The releasing ritual

Research on closure rituals – including studies where people physically discard written worries – shows that a deliberate, physical act of letting go produces a real sense of release, more than simply deciding to move on. The act has to be *active* and *intentional*.

How the app applies it:

- **You write to yourself first** (about 30 characters). You can't release what you haven't engaged with; the effort is the point.
- **Two taps, not one button** – crack, then shatter. The deliberateness gives it weight.
- **The scene is gone.** *"Your words are saved. The scene is gone."* The finality is what makes it feel real.

The spirit is *burn after writing*: the value is in the act of letting go, not in keeping the record.

## Two modes, because not everything needs to be burned

*Dark thoughts – Reflect & release* is the full ritual for something weighing on you. *Neutral notes – Save & keep* is for a moment you want to keep. Forcing a release on something you wanted to keep – or keeping something you needed to let go – would break the logic. See [Modes](features/modes.md).

## Why a stranger

- **A stranger has no stake and no memory.** They won't judge, won't bring it up later and don't need managing – the reason people sometimes confide more easily in a stranger on a train.
- **The stranger doesn't advise.** In the tradition of Motivational Interviewing, they reflect feelings back rather than prescribing solutions.
- **Anonymity is structural.** No account, no personal data, no identity. You can be honest because there is no self to protect.
- **No dependency.** The stranger is a tool for a moment of relief, not a companion. The app is not a substitute for human connection or clinical care.

## Design principles

Every part of Supportive Stranger is checked against these:

- **Lower every barrier to the first honest sentence** – short limit, gentle prompts, help for when you're stuck.
- **Put the effort in the middle, not at the ends** – starting is easy, writing to yourself takes real engagement, letting go feels earned.
- **Never lead your content** – help you find what's there, never suggest what should be there.
- **Protect the sense of being truly seen** – replies and images must feel specific to your words, not templated.
- **Respect the exit** – relief, release, close the app. No streaks, no nudges to come back.
- **A wellness tool, not a medical device** – see [Safety and wellbeing](safety.md).

## How the supporting features serve the ritual

- **Let's sort it out** helps people who arrive with diffuse stress and no words. Categorising is itself a form of labelling, and the prompt is a non-leading way in.
- **The pinned Sample Reflection** in the journal shows new users what a completed ritual looks and feels like. Its theme, loneliness, is near-universal.

Every feature exists to get you to that first honest sentence, and then through the arc to release.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Supportive Stranger based on research?

It draws on established ideas: expressive writing (James Pennebaker's research), externalisation from art therapy, studies on closure rituals, and the reflective listening style of Motivational Interviewing. The app itself is a wellness tool, not a clinical treatment.

### Why is there a 100-word limit?

To lower the barrier to starting and keep you close to the feeling. The goal is the first honest sentence, not a long diary entry.

### Why do I have to destroy the image?

A deliberate, physical act of letting go creates a stronger sense of release than simply deciding to move on. Writing to yourself first and then cracking and shattering the scene makes the release intentional.

### Why is the app designed not to keep me engaged?

Because its purpose is relief, not habit. The ritual ends, and you close the app.
